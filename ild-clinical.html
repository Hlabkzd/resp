Interstitial Lung Disease (ILD)

Also known as

Diffuse Parenchymal Lung Disease (DPLD)

Definition

ILD consists of a diverse group of disorders that involve the pulmonary parenchyma and interfere with gas exchange. The term "interstitial" reflects the pathologic appearance — the abnormality begins in the interstitium, though in advanced disease the alveoli are also involved.

Clinical Pearl

Two of the most important forms of ILD in clinical practice are IPF (Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis) and Sarcoidosis. Both can be familial but this is rare.

Causes & Classification of ILD

ILD has many causes; classified as unknown, known, granulomatous, or miscellaneous.

1. Unknown Causes (Idiopathic)

Most Common Idiopathic
Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPF)

Defined as a spontaneously occurring, specific form of chronic fibrosing interstitial pneumonia limited to the lung. IPF is associated with the characteristic Usual Interstitial Pneumonia (UIP) pattern on radiology and histology: fibroblastic foci and architectural distortion (honeycombing).

Other Idiopathic Types
  • NSIP — Non-Specific Interstitial Pneumonia
  • COP — Cryptogenic Organising Pneumonia
  • Acute Interstitial Pneumonia

2. Known Causes

Occupational / Environmental

Exposure to inorganic or organic dusts:

  • Silica (stone cutting)
  • Coal dust (coal mining)
  • Asbestos
  • Bird racing / farming (organic dusts)
Drug-Induced
NitrofurantoinAntibiotic (UTIs)
MethotrexateAnti-inflammatory (RA)
AmiodaroneAnti-arrhythmic
BleomycinChemotherapy
Radiation-Induced

Radiation-induced lung injury is directly related to the volume of irradiated lung and the cumulative dose of radiation. May occur weeks to years after treatment.

Connective Tissue Diseases
  • Rheumatoid Arthritis
  • Systemic Lupus Erythematosus
  • Scleroderma
  • Mixed Connective Tissue Disease

3. Granulomatous ILD

Sarcoidosis

A multi-system disease characterised by non-caseating granulomatous inflammation. It can mimic many other conditions due to its systemic nature — consider it as a differential in many settings.

4. Miscellaneous Causes

  • Infective causes — e.g. Tuberculosis (TB)
  • Malignancy — Lymphangitic carcinomatosis
  • Histiocytosis X (Langerhans cell histiocytosis)

Pathophysiology of ILD

Key Principle

Pathogenesis varies by underlying cause. IPF is dominated by fibrosis with little or no inflammation; most other ILDs begin with inflammation and develop fibrosis later.

IPF / UIP Pattern

Usual Interstitial Pneumonia (UIP) — the histological pattern in IPF — is characterised by:

  • Fibroblastic foci
  • Architectural distortion — "honeycombing" on HRCT and histology
  • Fibrosis with little or no inflammation

Other ILDs

Most other causes of ILD are predominated by inflammation and only develop fibrosis in later stages. This distinction is important — inflammatory ILDs may respond to immunosuppression, while established fibrosis in IPF does not.

Consequences of Interstitial Damage

Gas Exchange Failure

Interstitial thickening → reduced diffusing capacity for oxygen across the alveolar-capillary membrane → hypoxia.

Restrictive Defect

Progressive fibrosis → stiffening of the lung → reduced compliance → restricted movement → reduced lung volumes (FVC, TLC ↓).

Complication
Cor Pulmonale

Advanced ILD → chronic hypoxia → pulmonary vasoconstriction → pulmonary hypertension → right ventricular dysfunction (cor pulmonale). Also occurs in COPD.

Symptoms of ILD

Respiratory Symptoms

Cardinal
  • Progressive dyspnoea — the hallmark symptom
  • Dry cough — non-productive
Systemic
  • Fever, weight loss (CTD / sarcoidosis)
  • Eye symptoms — iritis, dry eyes
  • Joint pain (arthritis)
  • Skin rash
Sarcoidosis — Löfgren's Syndrome

Most common symptomatic presentation of sarcoidosis:

  • Fever
  • Lymphadenopathy
  • Arthralgia
  • Erythema nodosum — tender nodules on the shins

History-Taking in ILD

A careful history often reveals the cause. Cover all four domains:

1. Drug History

Detailed drug history is essential — drug-induced pulmonary fibrosis can occur weeks to years after the drug has been discontinued.

Key culprits: Nitrofurantoin · Methotrexate · Amiodarone · Bleomycin

2. Radiation History

Ask about previous malignancy treated with radiotherapy or chemotherapy. Injury is related to volume of irradiated lung and cumulative dose.

3. Occupational History

A lifelong employment history is essential. Review both home and work environments.

Examples: coal mining, stone cutting, farming, bird racing.

4. Smoking & Family History
  • Smoking is an independent risk factor for IPF
  • RBILD, DIP, and Langerhans' Cell Histiocytosis (LCH) occur almost exclusively in smokers
  • Both sarcoidosis and IPF can be familial (but rare)

Examination Tip

An important part of examining a patient with undiagnosed ILD is looking for extrapulmonary evidence of systemic disease — sarcoidosis and CTDs are common culprits.

Clinical Features — Explained

Fine Velcro Crackles

Crackles are interrupted, non-musical sounds resulting from collapse of peripheral airways on expiration. On inspiration, air rapidly enters these distal airways and the alveoli and small bronchi open abruptly, producing the crackling noise.

Think: the sound of gently separating the joined strip of velcro on a blood pressure cuff.

Fine late inspiratory crackles are characteristic of pulmonary fibrosis.

Hypoxia → Dyspnoea, Tachypnoea, Cyanosis

Interstitial thickening impairs oxygen diffusion → hypoxia, which drives:

  • Dyspnoea (shortness of breath)
  • Tachypnoea (rapid breathing — compensatory)
  • Cyanosis — central + peripheral
Clubbing

The exact mechanism is not fully understood. A proposed theory: growth factors from megakaryocytes and platelets lodge in nail bed capillaries and stimulate vascular connective tissue growth.

In ILD, clubbing is typically a late manifestation and suggests advanced fibrosis.

Cor Pulmonale — Mechanism

Advanced ILD → chronic hypoxia → hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction → pulmonary hypertension → increased RV afterload → right ventricular dysfunction (cor pulmonale).

This is the same mechanism as in COPD — any cause of chronic hypoxia can lead to cor pulmonale.

Exam Tip

Fine late inspiratory crackles are characteristic of pulmonary fibrosis. Coarse crackles heard in mid-inspiration suggest fluid (pulmonary oedema / heart failure) or secretions (bronchiectasis).
